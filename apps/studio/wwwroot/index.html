Mastering

Move your mix toward a song you love.

Give it your song and a song you wish yours sounded like. Both get split into their separate instruments, then your bass is compared with theirs, your drums with theirs, and so on — and you get told what that song does differently, one difference at a time, with a control for each.

  1. Your song and, if you have one, a reference.
  2. Both are split into vocals, drums, bass, guitar, piano and the rest. This is the slow part — roughly the length of each song, once.
  3. Instrument by instrument, you see what differs and try the suggestions you like. Nothing is applied until you say so, and every one can be undone.
  4. The drums go one level further if you want them to — your kick against their kick, and the snare, cymbals and toms separately.
  5. Then the whole mix is compared for the finishing moves, and you export.

Say what you want

moves the real controls

It reads a vocabulary rather than English, so it will say when it has not understood rather than quietly doing nothing.

What each record plays

Where the kick, the snare and the hats fall across the bar, how far from the grid they sit, the swing, and whether the bass gets out of the kick's way. Nothing here has a dial behind it — where a record's hats sit against the beat is a fact about a performance, and moving yours there would be composition rather than mastering.

Instrument by instrument

Your bass against that song's bass, your drums against its drums — level, tone in five bands, how evenly each part plays, where it sits and how wide it is. Each difference gets its own control, so you can take the air on the drums and leave everything else alone. The drums then go one level further, into your kick against their kick, and the snare, cymbals and toms separately.

Moving these is audible straight away — no re-render. The monitor runs the same band gains as the export, but its filters and compressor are the browser's, so it is a close preview rather than the finished master. Press Master for the real thing.

Why these settings?

This does not change the MP3. MP3 has a bitrate, not a bit depth — the encoder takes 16-bit and there is nothing on that path to choose. A deeper WAV is worth having when the master is going back into another tool, and makes no audible difference to a finished file.

What these do The short version of why each control exists.

Brightness is a shelf on top of the matched tone — the match can only give you the reference's top end, not more of it. Width spreads everything above 250 Hz and leaves the bass centered, so it survives a mono system. Extra headroom sits further under the reference; the master already stops short of over-driving its own limiter.

What this server can actually do

extract0r degrades rather than failing: when an engine is missing it falls back to a stub and carries on. Convenient, and also a trap — so this reports what is installed right now, not what the configuration asked for.
